### broad-mongodb-schema-design (all): Pass, top score 0.8386

**Q:** What should I consider when designing a MongoDB schema?

*Note in test-questions.json:* Spread over several sections: embed or reference, design patterns, anti-patterns, indexes, transactions.

> **Core rule:** Data that is accessed together should be stored together. [1] For read-heavy workloads with a clear access pattern, one document per read is simpler and usually faster. [1]
>
> **Flexibility and validation:** Documents in the same collection don't need identical fields, but a collection can still enforce rules with JSON Schema validation, such as required fields, types and allowed values. [2]
>
> **Patterns to consider:**
> - Bucket: group many small items into one document per time window. [3]
> - Computed: store an expensive value, such as an average rating, and update it on write. [3]
> - Subset: keep the most-used part of a large list in the main document and the rest in a separate collection. [3]
> - Attribute: turn many optional fields into an array of key-value pairs so one index covers them. [3]
> - Outlier: handle rare exceptional documents with an overflow document and a flag. [3]
> - Schema versioning: add a `schemaVersion` field so old and new shapes can coexist during gradual migration. [3]
>
> **Anti-patterns to avoid:**
> - **Unbounded arrays:** they keep the document growing toward the 16 MB limit and slow down every read of it. [4]
> - **Treating MongoDB like a relational database:** splitting every entity into its own collection and joining with `$lookup` on every read gives you the costs of both models and the benefits of neither. [4]
> - **Too many collections:** each with its own indexes, they waste memory. [4]
> - **Unnecessary indexes:** every index slows down writes and uses memory. [4]
> - **Case-insensitive regex queries:** a regular expression with the `i` flag can't use an index efficiently, so create an index with a case-insensitive collation instead. [4]

>

>    - Reduce the records to sort: move `$sort` as late as possible and make sure earlier stages significantly cut the number of records reaching it. [2]
>
